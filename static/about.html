RustCaptcha · About

Why am I seeing this?

RustCaptcha is a verification check that helps the site you're visiting tell humans apart from automated traffic. It runs in your browser and doesn't ask you to solve unrelated puzzles or identify objects in photos.

What it does

When you load a page that uses RustCaptcha, the widget asks the server for a small task. Depending on how risky the request looks, the server picks one of these:

  • Silent verification. Most visitors never see a checkbox — your browser solves a short proof-of-work puzzle in the background and the page just continues.
  • "I'm not a robot" checkbox. A single click; the actual proof-of-work runs after you click.
  • Image text challenge. If the request looks unusually risky, you may be asked to type the characters shown in a small image.

What proof-of-work means

Your browser performs a small computation — finding a number that, when hashed, has a certain pattern. It's free for one human, but expensive at scale, which is what discourages automated abuse. Solving it doesn't reveal anything about you; only the answer is sent back.

Privacy-respecting by design

RustCaptcha doesn't load tracking pixels, doesn't share data with advertising networks, and doesn't fingerprint your browser beyond the bare minimum needed to score the request. See the privacy notice for details on what is and isn't collected.

Open source & self-hosted

RustCaptcha is open-source software. The site you're visiting runs its own copy on its own servers — no third party sees your check. The operator of this site is responsible for the terms under which it's used.
